# UncivWarcraft

A standalone base ruleset that turns Unciv into the world of Warcraft: the races of Azeroth as playable factions, a tech tree that runs through Azeroth's history, and Warcraft wonders, units and resources.

**Status: alpha.** Every race has its own leader, ability, cities and AI, plus unique units and a unique building (the Humans have the most). Every race has its own look on the map, though: each foot, mounted and crewed unit is redrawn per race (bearded, stocky Dwarves; small Gnomes; tall, long-eared elves; tusked Trolls in varied skin tones; green, muscular Orcs; horned Tauren and Draenei; Pandaren; Worgen; Forsaken; Goblins). Mounted units ride each race's own beast: wolves, rams, mechanostriders, nightsabers, kodos, raptors, hawkstriders, elekks, dragon turtles, goblin trikes, skeletal horses, white steeds and Gilnean horses.

## What's in it

**Eras** follow Azeroth's timeline: Age of Tribes → Age of Arathor → Age of the Dark Portal → Age of the Third War → Age of the Lich King → Age of the Cataclysm → Age of the Legion → Age of Azerite → Age of the Void.

**21 races**, each with a leader, unique ability, AI personality and city list: Humans, Orcs, Night Elves, Dwarves, Gnomes, Tauren, Trolls, Blood Elves, High Elves, Pandaren, Forsaken, Draenei, Goblins, Worgen, **the Dark Iron Dwarves** under Moira Thaurissan in Shadowforge City, **the Kul Tirans** under Jaina Proudmoore in Boralus, **the Zandalari Trolls** under Queen Talanji in Zuldazar, and **the Scourge** under Arthas, the Lich King, ruling from Icecrown Citadel in Northrend. From the Broken Isles and the deep come **the Nightborne** of Suramar under First Arcanist Thalyssra, **the Highmountain Tauren** under Mayla Highmountain in Thunder Totem, and **the Naga** under Queen Azshara, rising from sunken Nazjatar.

**Humans (complete)** — led by Anduin Wrynn, in Stormwind blue and gold
- *The Human Spirit:* City-State influence decays 50% slower and rests 20 higher; +15% Great Person generation. *For Lordaeron!* +10% Strength in your own lands. *Call to Arms:* when another civilization declares war on you, 2 free infantry of your current era appear.
- **Footman** (replaces Swordsman): stronger, starts with Defend I, needs no Iron; carries a heater shield with a sunburst.
- **Paladin** (replaces Knight): plate armour and warhammer on a barded white charger. Heals adjacent units, +10% Strength to nearby units (Devotion Aura), +20% when defending (Divine Shield), +25% against the Forsaken.
- **Mage** (replaces Crossbowman): a bearded battle-mage hurling fire from both hands; stronger ranged attack that fires over hills and forests.
- **Priest** (replaces Missionary): white-and-gold robes; adjacent units heal 15 HP more.
- **Steam Tank** (replaces Cannon): no set-up before firing, and can move after attacking.
- **Knight of the Silver Hand** (replaces Cavalry): heavy holy cavalry; +25% against the undead, and units beside it heal 10 HP more.
- **Archmage** (replaces Great Scientist): named after the archmagi of Dalaran (Antonidas, Khadgar, Jaina…).
- **Chapel of the Light** (replaces Temple): +1 Faith, +1 Culture, +1 Happiness. **Royal Barracks** (replaces Barracks): new units start with 25 XP, +1 Culture. **Lumber Mill** (replaces Workshop): +1 Production, +1 Production from Forests. **Castle** (replaces Keep): stronger defenses, +1 Culture. **Cathedral of Light** (replaces Bardic Hall): +2 Faith. **Altar of Kings** (Humans only, one per empire): a free Great General and +15% Great Person generation.
- Every Human and Orc unit is painted in a chunky, Warcraft-like style: big shoulders, hands and weapons, saturated colour and heavy outlines, from Peasants and Peons to riflemen, riders, siege engines, Great People and heroes.

**Each race plays differently:**
- **Scourge:** the undead eat half as much, Plagued Land gives them +2 Production and +1 Science, fallen enemies can rise to swell their cities, and their key buildings (Necropolis, Crypt, Ziggurat) need blighted ground nearby. Acolytes **spread the blight**. Unique: Ghoul, Acolyte, Death Knight, Frost Wyrm.
- **Forsaken:** Plagued Land feeds them and heals their units; they can spread it too.
- **Night Elves:** forests give +1 Food and are never cut down; **Wisps** replace Peasants.
- **Orcs:** +10% Production and Strength while at war, -10% Culture in peace.
- **Trolls:** regeneration (+10 HP when healing), +1 Faith from jungle, an extra Follower belief.
- **Tauren:** untouched land is sacred: unfarmed plains give Faith, hills without mines give Culture.
- **Dwarves:** **Deep Mines** (+1 Gold) replace Mines, Titan Ruins give Science and Culture, and some ancient ruins hold Titan relics only dwarves can read.
- **Gnomes:** their own experiment events, with big breakthroughs or explosions.
- **Goblins:** buying costs 20% less Gold and captured cities pay out, but Production is 10% lower.
- **Blood Elves:** mana addiction: -1 Happiness per city from the Age of the Third War until they control Arcane Crystals or the Sunwell is restored; each Magisters' Sanctum eases it (+1 Happiness).
- **High Elves:** cities are 25% stronger and grow 10% faster.
- **Draenei:** exiles: a second Settler, +25% against barbarians and demons, faster missionaries.
- **Worgen:** units shift between human and worgen form (Worgen Stalker ↔ Gilnean Hunter, Gilnean Rifleman ↔ Worgen Ravager), and the curse lets worgen capture units they kill.
- **Pandaren:** can embark from the start; in the Age of the Dark Portal they choose the Tushui path (Alliance), the Huojin path (Horde) or neutrality.
- **Dark Iron Dwarves:** Molten Fields feed their forges (+1 Gold), mined hills give +1 Production, and their siege engines are built 20% faster and fight 10% harder.
- **Kul Tirans:** the Proudmoore Admiralty: ships move one tile farther, fight 15% harder, see farther and are built 25% faster; coast tiles give +1 Gold.
- **Zandalari Trolls:** an empire of gold: +1 Gold from jungle, +10% Gold and +15% Faith; jungle camps give Faith and Culture, and their dinosaur riders fight 10% harder.
- **Alliance and Horde** fight 10% harder against each other.

**Heroes:** every race can field one hero, as in Warcraft III: Uther the Lightbringer, Grom Hellscream, Malfurion Stormrage, Muradin Bronzebeard, Kelsey Steelspark, Baine Bloodhoof, Rokhan, Kael'thas Sunstrider, Alleria Windrunner, Chen Stormstout, Nathanos Blightcaller, Maraad, Gazlowe, Darius Crowley, Kel'Thuzad, Dagran Thaurissan, Daelin Proudmoore and Rastakhan. Heroes inspire nearby troops, grow stronger with the ages and have their own promotions.

**Magic and travel:** **Mana** is a resource every race needs. Every capital gathers a little each turn, and each race has its own sources in buildings it already has: Moonwells, Voodoo Lounges, the Orcs' new Altar of Storms, Great Totems, Dwarven Forges, Tinker's Workshops, Goblin Bazaars, Crystal Sanctums, Necropolises and Ziggurats, Arcane Sanctums and Mage Towers (more with Arcane Crystals) and more. It is spent on each race's spellcasters and magic-touched units, on every hero (30 Mana), on magical buildings and wonders (Mage Tower, Arcane Laboratory, Mana Reactor, Karazhan, the Sunwell Plateau, the Nighthold...), on Mana choices in events (sealing a fel rift, warding granaries against the plague, holding back the Scourge), on surplus Mana whenever the ley lines swell (a civ holding 300+ Mana can turn it into Science, a Golden Age, or Culture and Faith), and on portals: units trained in a city with a **Mage Tower** can open a portal from one of your cities to another for 10 Mana.

**Cleansing the land:** Plagued Land and Fel-Scorched Land can only be cleansed by a **Cleric**, bought with Faith. Every living race has its own: Northshire Cleric, Dwarven Priest, Decontaminator (Gnomes), Cenarion Druid, Anchorite, Harvest Witch, Lightwarden, Sunfury Priest, Earthen Shaman, Witch Doctor, Sunwalker, Mistweaver, the Goblins' Hazmat Crew, the Dark Irons' Flamekeeper, the Kul Tiran Tidesage and the Zandalari Loa Speaker. The undead don't cleanse.

**World threats:** the Scourge marches in the Age of the Lich King, the Legion invades in the Age of the Legion, and the Old Gods whisper in the last ages. The red, blue, bronze, green and black dragonflights may visit with gifts or schemes.

**Unique units and buildings for every race:**
- **Orcs:** Grunt (Swordsman; heals when it kills), Wolf Rider (Outrider; pillages for free, no penalty against cities), Raider (Cavalry; chains and hooks: no penalty against cities, strong against riders), Pig Farm (Granary, +1 Production), War Mill (Workshop; +1 Production, faster military units).
- **Trolls:** Headhunter (Ranger; stronger and faster in jungle), Troll Berserker (Musketeer; frenzied, heals on kills), Batrider (Gyrocopter; strong against land units), Voodoo Lounge (Shrine, +1 Gold, +1 Culture), Loa Shrine (Temple, +1 Culture).
- **Tauren:** Tauren Warrior (Vanguard; much stronger, no Iron), Kodo Beast (Mounted Archer; War Drums aura), Wind Rider (Gyrocopter; longer range), Great Totem (Town Hall, +1 Faith), Spirit Lodge (Herb Garden, +1 Faith, +1 Food, +1 Happiness).
- **Forsaken:** Abomination (Vanguard; tough, heals on kills, no Iron), Meat Wagon (Catapult; faster), Deathstalker (Rifleman; ignores zones of control), Apothecarium (Herb Garden, +2 Science), Plague Cauldron (Foundry, +1 Science).
- **Blood Elves:** Spellbreaker (Swordsman; strong against archers and mages), Blood Knight (Knight; heals on kills), Dragonhawk Rider (Gyrocopter; strong against land units), Magisters' Sanctum (Arcane Sanctum, +1 Science, +1 Culture), Mana Forge (Foundry, +1 Production, +1 Mana).
- **Goblins:** Goblin Shredder (Peasant; faster, sees farther), Goblin Sapper (Cannon; devastating against cities), Goblin Zeppelin (Bomber Zeppelin; strong against cities), Goblin Bazaar (Market, +2 Gold), Trade Prince's Bank (Bank, +2 Gold).
- **Dwarves:** Ironforge Rifleman (Musketeer; strong in hills), Ram Rider (Knight; keeps terrain defense, strong in hills), Steam Armor (War Golem; strong in hills), Dwarven Forge (Blacksmith, +2 Production), Great Forge (Foundry, +2 Production).
- **Gnomes:** Mechanostrider Rider (Outrider; 5 movement, no Horses), Gnomish Bombardier (Mortar Team; range 3), Spider Tank (Siege Engine; faster, ignores terrain), Tinker's Workshop (Workshop, +1 Science), Gnomeregan Laboratory (Arcane Laboratory, +2 Science).
- **Night Elves:** Huntress (Outrider; strong and fast in forests), Glaive Thrower (Ballista; range 3), Mountain Giant (War Golem; living stone, no Azerite, strong in forests), Moonwell (Shrine, +1 Food), Tree of Life (Keep, +2 Food, stronger).
- **Draenei:** Vindicator (Vanguard; strong against barbarians and demons, heals neighbours), Elekk Rider (Knight; very strong but slow), Lightforged Warframe (War Golem; heals neighbours), Crystal Sanctum (Temple, +1 Science), Crystal Hall (Library, +1 Faith).
- **Worgen:** Worgen Stalker (Swordsman; strong and fast in forests, no Iron), Gilnean Rifleman (Rifleman), Harvest Golem (War Golem; strong defender), Gilnean Bulwark (Walls, stronger, +1 Culture), Greymane Watchtower (Keep, +1 Culture, stronger).
- **High Elves:** Farstrider (Ranger; strong in forests), Ranger-Lord (Mountaineer; strong in forests), Elven Destroyer (War Galley; stronger and faster), Runestone (Walls, stronger, +1 Science), Elven Spire (Mage Tower, +1 Culture, +1 Happiness).
- **Pandaren:** Brewmaster (Vanguard; strong defender, no Iron), Shado-Pan Monk (Pikeman; 3 movement), Cloud Serpent Rider (Gryphon Rider; heals faster), Brewery (Granary, +1 Gold, +1 Happiness), Jade Pagoda (Temple, +1 Culture, +1 Happiness).
- **Scourge:** Crypt Fiend (Mountaineer; webs flying units, heals on kills), Slaughterhouse (Foundry, +1 Production; needs blight).
- **Dark Iron Dwarves:** Fireguard (Pikeman; strong in hills and on defence), Dark Iron Golem (War Golem; hits harder), Shadowforge Furnace (Blacksmith, +2 Production).
- **Kul Tirans:** Kul Tiran Man-o'-War (Frigate; faster and better armed), Admiralty Gunner (Musketeer; attacks from ships without penalty), Admiralty Drydock (Shipyard; +1 Production, +1 Happiness, ships start with 15 XP).
- **Zandalari Trolls:** Raptor Rider (Outrider; fast, strong in jungle), Direhorn Rider (Knight; a battering charge with no penalty against cities), Golden Pyramid (Market, +1 Gold, +1 Culture).
Each has its own map sprite and icon.

**Azeroth world maps:** the whole world as on Blizzard's in-game world map: Kalimdor, the Eastern Kingdoms, Northrend, **Pandaria**, the **Broken Isles**, **Kul Tiras** and **Zandalar**, with 89 zones in their real places and shapes (Elwynn Forest, the Barrens, Dun Morogh, Tanaris, Icecrown, Kun-Lai Summit, Suramar, Drustvar, Vol'dun and the rest), traced from the in-game continent maps. Mountain ranges, lakes, rivers, the Maelstrom, Kezan, the Lost Isles, the Wandering Isle, the Isle of Thunder, the Timeless Isle and the Isle of Giants. Each continent is drawn a little larger than on the in-game map so the seas between them leave room to play. Three sizes: **Azeroth Huge** (180 × 110, preselected), **Azeroth Large** (144 × 88) and **Azeroth** (104 × 64, faster on phones); plus regional maps of **Kalimdor**, the **Eastern Kingdoms**, **Northrend**, **Pandaria** and the **Broken Isles** cut from the same world. Pandaria and the Broken Isles have their own landmarks: the Peak of Serenity on **Mount Neverest**, the amber of **Kypari Zar**, the **Tomb of Sargeras**, the great tree **Shaladrassil** and **Neltharion's Lair**. Each race starts at its capital: Stormwind, Orgrimmar, Darnassus, Ironforge, Gnomeregan, Thunder Bluff, Sen'jin Village, Silvermoon, Quel'Danas, the Temple of Five Dawns on the Wandering Isle, Undercity, the Exodar, Bilgewater Harbor, Gilneas, Shadowforge City, Boralus and Zuldazar. City-states sit at their real locations.

**Warcraft terrain:** seven special tile types, placed where they belong:
- *Fel-Scorched Land* (Felwood, Blasted Lands): +2 Production, -1 Food; units ending a turn there take 10 damage. Clerics can cleanse it after Priesthood.
- *Plagued Land* (the Plaguelands, Ghostlands, Icecrown, Dragonblight, Zul'Drak): -1 Food, -1 Production until a Cleric cleanses it.
- *Ancient Woods* (Ashenvale, Darkshore, Teldrassil, Moonglade, Hyjal, Feralas, Azshara): night elf forest, +1 Culture, can't be cut down.
- *Bamboo Grove* (the Wandering Isle): 2 Food, 1 Production.
- *Molten Fields* (Burning Steppes, Searing Gorge, Un'Goro): +2 Production, -1 Food, rough going.
- *Crystal Grove* (Crystalsong Forest, Azuremyst and Bloodmyst): +2 Science.
- *Titan Ruins* (Uldaman, Uldum, the Storm Peaks, Sholazar): +1 Science, +1 Culture.

**20 landmarks** (natural wonders) with their own tile art: the Maelstrom, Mount Hyjal, Teldrassil, Moonglade, Lake Elune'ara, Stonetalon Peak, the Red Rocks, Thousand Needles, Un'Goro Crater, the Lakkari Tar Pits, the Lost City of the Tol'vir, Blackrock Mountain, the Cape of Stranglethorn, Kelp'thar Forest, and new: **the Dark Portal**, **the Nexus**, **Galakrond's Rest**, **the Sunken Temple**, **Maraudon** and **the Lifeblood Pillar**.

**City centers in each race's style**, growing from village (Age of Tribes) to town (Age of the Dark Portal) to walled city (Age of the Cataclysm): blue-roofed Stormwind stone and a cathedral for the Humans, spiked red-and-black halls for the Orcs, violet leaf-roofed temples under a great tree for the Night Elves, a forge-lit mountain gate for the Dwarves, gear-and-dome Gnomish workshops, painted tents and a mesa for the Tauren, thatched huts and a stone ziggurat for the Trolls, red-and-gold spires and a golden-ringed tower for the Blood Elves, white-and-blue spires for the High Elves, pagodas for the Pandaren, Lordaeron's ruined keep with fel-green windows for the Forsaken, crystal spires for the Draenei, a goblin bank with smokestacks and a crane, and Gilnean manors with gas lamps for the Worgen. City-states match their founders (Dalaran is violet, Gadgetzan and Booty Bay goblin, Cenarion Hold night elf), and no city ever turns into a modern skyscraper.

**Original attack sounds:** no machine guns, tank shots, jets or missiles. Fifteen sounds made for the mod (synthesised in code, free to share) give black-powder rifles, steam cannons, gyrocopter guns, gnomish mortars, goblin bombs and rockets, arcane blasts, mana bombs, broadsides and titan lightning, plus the roar of the frost wyrm, the gryphon's cry, the batrider's shriek, the worgen's snarl and the infernal's crash.

**Ships, camps and forts per race:** every race sails its own ships (orcish hide sails and spikes, night elf leaf prows and moon lanterns, pandaren junk sails, Forsaken tatters and green lanterns, Scourge bone and saronite, gnomish and goblin smokestacks, Blood Elf crimson and gold…), and its hunting camps and forts are built in its own architecture (spiked palisades, thorn walls, pagoda gates, saronite ramparts, riveted goblin scrap) on the HexaRealm tiles. There are no submarines.

**No modern leftovers:** late-era units are Warcraft machines and soldiers rather than tanks and jets: steam golems, dwarven siege engines, gnomish mechano-tanks, mechagnomes, an iron colossus, Horde demolishers, goblin rocket wagons, gnomish flying machines, ironclads and steam warships, an airship tender, an armoured war turtle and an orcish juggernaught (there are no submarines), plus musket-and-steel infantry (mountaineers, fusiliers, grenadiers, Kul Tiran marines, rocket-pack skyguards). The Titan vessel is built from titan-forged parts. All technology, building, wonder, unit, resource, improvement, policy and air/naval promotion icons are Warcraft-themed. Azerite, Arcane Crystals, Elementium and Thorium have their own map art, the drills are goblin rigs, and farms, mines and forts keep their pre-industrial look in every era. Resources and beliefs use Warcraft names (Kodo, Plainstriders, Star Rubies, Tel'Abim Bananas, Glowcap, Mammoth Ivory, Moonglow Ink, Deeprock Salt; An'she's Light, Neptulon's Tide, Rage of Agamaggan, Whispers of Elune…), and every wonder, technology and unit has a lore entry in the civilopedia. The social policy tree is Warcraft too: Old Kingdoms, Frontier, Blood and Thunder, Devotion, Diplomacy, Trade Princes, Arcane Inquiry, Free Peoples, Dominion and Unity, with policies such as Strom's Legacy, Ready to Work, Lok'tar Ogar, Kor'kron Regulars, Kirin Tor Exchange, Steamwheedle Charter, Patrons of Dalaran and Iron Horde Doctrine.

**Warcraft events:** fifteen random events from Warcraft lore pop up with choices: the **Darkmoon Faire**, the **Plague of Undeath** in your granaries, **Legion Incursions** (a fel rift opens and Felguards or Infernals pour out), **a Goblin Offer** from the Steamwheedle Cartel, the **Lunar Festival**, **Brewfest**, **Children's Week**, **Hallow's End**, **Gnomish Refugees** from Gnomeregan, **a Titan Vault** found by the Explorers' League, **the Cataclysm** (only in the Age of the Cataclysm), **a Wandering Brewmaster**, **an Envoy of the Kirin Tor**, **Murloc Raids** and **the Defias Brotherhood**. Each belongs to its part of the timeline; on average a civilization sees one every 15 turns or so.

**Barbarians are Azeroth's raiders:** murloc packs and gnoll warbands in the early ages, then the red-masked Defias Brotherhood, all with their own sprites; their camps are Defias hideouts dug into old mines. The Burning Legion's **Felguard** and **Infernal** only appear through Legion Incursions.

**Every race's Great People carry that race's names:** an Orc general may be Varok Saurfang or Nazgrim, a Dwarf scientist Brann Bronzebeard, a Goblin merchant Baron Revilgaz, a Night Elf prophet Tyrande Whisperwind. The Great Person improvements are the **Arcane Academy**, **Trade Company**, **Bastion**, **Hall of Heroes** and **Sacred Grounds**; specialists are **Arcanists**, **Traders**, **Bards** and **Tinkers**; city-states are Lorekeeper, Harbor, Mercantile, Militaristic or Sacred, and each speaks with its own voice (Dalaran, Booty Bay, Light's Hope Chapel…). Promotions use Warcraft names too (Deadeye, Heroic Strike, Weapon Mastery, Defend, Spear Wall, Bloodlust, Rapid Fire, Broadsides, Depth Stalker, Skyguard Patrol, Sky Sentinel), ancient ruins and city-state quests are reworded for Azeroth, and every building, resource, improvement, belief, policy and promotion now has a civilopedia entry.

**Faction AI:** every race has its own AI personality. Orcs and Forsaken mass armies, Gnomes and Dwarves chase technology and siege engines, the elves and Draenei favor culture and faith, Goblins build markets and banks, Humans stay balanced. Alliance races are more diplomatic and loyal, so they tend to befriend one another more often; Horde races are quicker to go to war.

**28 city-states:** the other peoples of Azeroth — the furbolgs of **Timbermaw Hold**, the tuskarr of **Kamagua**, the centaurs of **Maraudine**, the kobolds of **Jasperlode Mine**, the quilboar of **Razorfen Kraul**, the ethereals of **the Consortium**, the mogu of **Mogu'shan** and the qiraji of **Ahn'Qiraj** — alongside Dalaran, Theramore, Stromgarde, Gadgetzan, Booty Bay, Ratchet, Everlook, Steamwheedle Port, Light's Hope Chapel, Cenarion Hold, Argent Vanguard, Feathermoon Stronghold, the Earthen Ring, the Knights of the Ebon Blade, the Explorers' League and the Thorium Brotherhood. Pandaria has the **Shado-Pan Monastery**, the **Golden Lotus** and the mantid of **Klaxxi'vess**, and the Broken Isles have the vrykul of **Valdisdall**, with Dalaran floating over Azsuna as it did in Legion.

**12 religions:** Holy Light, Elune, Earth Mother, Shamanism, Loa, Cenarion Circle, August Celestials, Titans, Forgotten Shadow, Light of the Sunwell, the Old Gods and the Nightwell.

**Warcraft names throughout**, drawn from the Warcraft II/III strategy games and World of Warcraft: techs (Iron, Steel and Mithril Forged Swords, Animal War Training, Long Rifles, Storm Hammers, Liquid Fire, Eye of Kilrogg…), units (Peasant, Militia, Ballista, Gryphon Rider, Mortar Team, Siege Engine, Demolisher, Flying Machine, Giant Turtle, Juggernaught…), buildings (Town Hall, Keep, Blacksmith, Arcane Sanctum, Mage Tower, Shipyard, Guard Tower, Beastiary, Auction House, Coin Forge, Hall of Relics…), policies and unit types without modern terms, 40 world wonders (Karazhan, Ulduar, Icecrown Citadel, Zul'Gurub…), resources (Thorium, Azerite, Mageweave, Truesilver…), and Great People named after each race's own heroes.

## Scenarios

Seven ready-made starts (pick *Scenario* when starting a new game):
- **The Third War**: the Scourge plague spreads through Lordaeron.
- **The Cataclysm**: Deathwing breaks the world.
- **Battle for Azeroth**: the Alliance and the Horde at war again over Azerite.
- **Alliance versus Horde**: eight Alliance peoples against eight Horde peoples on the whole world.
- **The Legion Invasion**: Gul'dan opens the Tomb of Sargeras and the Legion lands on the Broken Isles. The Alliance and the Horde stand with the Nightborne and Highmountain.
- **Mists of Pandaria**: the Alliance and the Horde carry their war to Pandaria while the Pandaren choose a side.
- **Wrath of the Lich King**: the Scourge against everyone in Northrend, with Kel'Thuzad and Sapphiron.

## Music

A royalty-free fantasy soundtrack ("Winds of Azeroth", five tracks) plays for every faction, whichever race you choose: four ambient tracks during the game and a shorter one on the main menu.

**Faction music slots:** each faction can have its own theme, peace and war music. Put a track in `music/factions/<Faction>/` with the mood (`Theme`, `Peace` or `War`) in its file name; the build names it the way Unciv expects and sets it to the same volume as the rest. See [`music/factions/README.md`](music/factions/README.md).

## Installing

**In Unciv (any device):** open *Mods*, search for **UncivWarcraft** and press *Download*. Or use *Download mod from URL* and paste this repository's GitHub address.

**Desktop, manually:** download this repository as a ZIP, unzip it, rename the folder to `UncivWarcraft`, and put it in Unciv's `mods` folder.

Then start a new game, choose **UncivWarcraft** as the base ruleset, and pick the *Custom* map type: the Azeroth map is preselected.

## Building from source

The mod is generated from Unciv's own Gods & Kings ruleset by the scripts in [`tools/`](tools/TOOLS.md), so it can be rebuilt for new Unciv versions. Edit the data files there (races, names, map layout) rather than the generated JSON.
